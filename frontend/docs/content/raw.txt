3 Days sounds long. Until you try building something real. 

In startups, the hardest part isn’t building.
It’s deciding what is worth building.
CodeVolt 2.0 places students in that exact space.
Over 3 Days, hundreds and hundreds of students work in parallel  across disciplines, ideas, and approaches, everybody trying to answer the different question in different way !!.
What emerges isn’t just MVPs.
It’s clarity.
You don’t leave with certainty.
You leave knowing your idea can stand in a room full of serious builders.
Teams experience the full arc of early startup building — problem selection, validation, trade-offs, execution, and defense.

Track: 
Startup Track
Build a startup in 3 Days. 
Offline. From Scratch. Real Problems. Real Solutions. 

Teams only. Limited seats. 

How it works: 
3 days. 3 Gates. One direction. 

Day 1 - The Idea

Identify a real problem
Question assumptions
Choose what not to build

*Clarity begins here.


Day 2 - The Product

Turn intent into an MVP
Validate with real use cases
Make trade-offs

*Execution exposes reality.


Day 3 - The Business

Define value
Define Users / Market
Working Product 

*Readiness matters.


Each gate removes uncertainty.
Each gate raises the bar.

Not every team reaches the final day.

How teams are evaluated
This is not about how much you build. It’s about what you choose to build  and why.

Teams are evaluated the way early startups are evaluated through decisions, clarity, and realism.
*No scores cards. No predefined checklists.

You should focus on 
Problem clarity | User understanding | Product thinking | Business sense | Decision ownership


What Matters More
Clear thinking beats complex features.
Honest validation beats assumptions.
Direction beats speed.

What Doesn’t Matter
Animated slides don’t help.
Buzzwords / Trending don’t help
Overbuilding doesn’t help.

AI can help you move faster.
It cannot decide for you. (there is a difference)
Teams that rely on auto-generated ideas or copied ideas will be filtered out.
CodeVolt 2.0 evaluates ownership, clarity of problem, intent, decisions, and the ability to defend why something should exist.
